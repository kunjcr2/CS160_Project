The survey points strongly toward a healthcare-first voice assistant: appointment booking, reminders, medication support, multilingual interaction, and explicit confirmation controls are the clearest themes.

Strong report-worthy trends
1. Appointment booking is the dominant feature.
12 of 13 respondents selected Doctor appointment booking as one of the three features to prioritize.
This is by far the strongest feature signal in the survey.
2. Reminders are the next major need.
Appointment reminders: 9/13 selected in their top three.
Medication reminders: 8/13 selected in their top three.
This supports focusing the product on healthcare management rather than general-purpose assistance.
3. The scheduling problem is actually happening.
7/13 respondents said the older adult had delayed/avoided a medical appointment at least once because the process was difficult or frustrating.
Another 3/13 were unsure.
This gives you direct evidence supporting the project's original problem statement.
4. Patient portals are the most common difficulty.
7/13 selected using patient portals or healthcare apps as a difficult task.
Other common problems:
Language barriers: 5/13
Remembering appointments: 5/13
Making/changing appointments: 4/13
Remembering medications: 4/13.
5. Multilingual support looks important, especially for non-English users.
Preferred languages included English, Spanish, Vietnamese, and Mandarin/Cantonese.
Importance of supporting the preferred language averaged roughly 4.4/5.
10/13 said "Yes" they would be more likely to use the product if it worked entirely in their preferred language; the remaining 3 said "Maybe."
Among the six respondents whose preferred language was not English, 5/6 rated multilingual voice support "Extremely useful."
6. Confirmation before actions is extremely important.
For actions involving money, 10/13 said they would allow it only if they approved every transaction.
Only one respondent said they would not allow it at all.
This strongly supports your existing Confirm-and-Undo / explicit confirmation design.
7. People are reasonably open to appointment automation, but trust isn't complete.
Doctor appointment automation:
3 Yes
5 Probably
4 Not sure
1 Probably not
0 outright No
So 8/13 are positively inclined, but confirmation and transparency should remain mandatory.
8. Proactive check-ins are much more controversial.
7/13: "It depends on how often."
2: positive.
3: negative/annoyed/did not want it.
1: unsure.
So don't make aggressive proactive conversation a core feature. Make the frequency configurable and easy to disable.
9. Caregiver access should be limited, not surveillance-heavy.
Upcoming appointments and medication status were commonly considered acceptable to share.
Only 1 respondent selected conversation transcripts.
Several explicitly chose "The user should choose exactly what is shared."
This strongly supports your existing idea of a caregiver dashboard without conversation transcripts.
10. Ride booking looks relatively weak.
Only 1/13 selected ride booking among their top three priorities.
Several respondents rated it Not useful or Slightly useful.
It looks like a reasonable feature to deprioritize for the first prototype.

This fits the project's goal of cutting the larger candidate feature set down to roughly 6–8 meaningful features.

One VERY important report limitation

All 13 respondents identified themselves as caregivers/supporters of someone aged 60+, not as the older adult themselves. So in the report, say "survey respondents/caregivers indicated...", not "older adults prefer...". Your project specifically intended to compare those two groups, but the current sample cannot make that comparison yet.
